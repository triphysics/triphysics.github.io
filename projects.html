Failure detection and prediction from test-rig sensor data

Problem
Performance tests of cutting-tool grades produce continuous instrument and sensor data. Failures need to be detected reliably, and ideally predicted before they happen.
Approach
Machine-learning and time-series models for failure detection and prediction, developed in Databricks. Alongside the modelling, I help integrate equipment, sensors and databases so data is collected in a structured way from the start.
Outcome
Analyses and visualisations of test data that I present to the engineers who run the tests. The work is ongoing, and the code is company property.
  • Python
  • Databricks
  • Time series
  • Anomaly detection
  • Sensor data

Machine-learning prediction models that know their limits

Problem
Prediction models trained on large scientific datasets can look accurate on average while failing badly on the cases that matter most.
Approach
End-to-end pipelines covering data generation, cleaning, feature preparation, training and evaluation. Models were validated against reference data, and I systematically mapped where their predictions could not be trusted.
Outcome
Validated models, plus a clear account of their reliable range, so collaborators knew when to use a prediction and when to fall back on a direct calculation.
  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • PyTorch
  • Model validation
  • Uncertainty

2019 to 2023

High-throughput data pipelines with built-in quality control

Problem
Screening hundreds of candidate materials by hand is slow and error-prone, and silent failures in individual runs can corrupt a whole dataset.
Approach
Automated pipelines that generate, integrate and check data across hundreds of cases, running unattended on supercomputers. Quality-control steps flag anomalies, outliers and failed runs, and every result carries structured metadata, following FAIR principles.
Outcome
Reusable, traceable datasets at a scale that justified a Flemish Tier-1 HPC allocation of about 35 million CPU-hours.

  • AiiDA
  • Atomate2
  • Bash
  • HPC

MX-PHon: a curated public database with a web front end

Problem
Researchers working on MXenes, a large family of 2D materials, had no single place to find consistent structural and vibrational data.
Approach
I curated crystal structures and phonon dispersions into one dataset and published it with a searchable web interface. Users can browse by metal, carbide or nitride, and surface group, or search for a compound directly.
Outcome
A public, openly hosted dataset that anyone can explore without installing anything.

PLATO and PyFlex: documented Python packages used by other researchers

Problem
Analyses that lived in personal scripts could not be reused or checked by anyone else.
Approach
I turned them into two packages: PLATO, a Python analysis toolkit, and PyFlex, a modelling framework. I designed, documented, released and maintained both.
Outcome
Tools that other researchers use in their own work, with documentation they can follow without me.
