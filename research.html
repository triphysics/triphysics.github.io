Research

The physics behind my data work. For ten years I combined first-principles calculations, Boltzmann transport theory and large-scale automated workflows to explain, predict and design how materials carry heat and charge, respond to strain, and order magnetically.

Thermal transport and thermoelectrics

Controlling how heat moves through a material matters for technologies from integrated circuits to power plants. Better thermoelectrics can recover electricity from waste heat; materials that guide heat flow can make lithium-ion batteries safer; and good thermal management improves solar cells and the energy efficiency of buildings.

Current focus

  • First-principles and Boltzmann transport methods for phonon scattering: phonon–phonon, phonon–electron and phonon–defect.
  • Phonons and anharmonic free energies in strongly anharmonic crystals.
  • Phonon topology and phonon angular momentum.

Electromechanical properties

Flexoelectricity couples electric polarisation to strain gradients. Unlike piezoelectricity it is allowed in every material, and it becomes much stronger in two-dimensional materials, which bend easily. Stacking or twisting 2D layers into heterostructures creates moiré patterns that offer a new way to tune these electromechanical responses.

Current focus

  • How strain gradients change quasiparticle excitations, optoelectronic response and transport.
  • Non-homogeneous strain and its effect on piezoelectricity and flexoelectricity.

Magnetism and magnetic materials

Permanent magnets

Strong permanent magnets are essential to electric motors, wind turbines and countless devices. Their performance relies on the large spin–orbit coupling of rare-earth f electrons, yet rare earths are costly and supply-constrained. With the Critical Materials Institute I worked on magnets that need less rare-earth content, and I plan to extend this with high-throughput searches for new candidates.

Two-dimensional magnets

In 2017, long-range magnetic order was found to survive down to a single layer in CrI3 and Cr2Ge2Te6. Since then, high-throughput and machine-learning studies have predicted many more 2D magnets. I am interested in controlling their magnetism with electric fields and in van der Waals heterostructures built from them.

Materials behaviour under high pressure

Pressure is a clean way to change interatomic distances without changing chemistry. I use first-principles calculations to follow crystal structure, phase relations and transport under extreme compression, working closely with experimental collaborators who use diamond anvil cells. Together we have shown that pressure can tune the optical and transport properties of layered materials.

Interests

  • Multilayer and nano-layered materials.
  • Superhard materials.
  • Transition-metal compounds and strongly correlated systems.
